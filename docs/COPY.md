Example copy:

We are proud to introduce the inaugural edition of Unprompted, King's Artificial Intelligence and
Philosophy Society's mag-journal.

Artificial Intelligence may be the most powerful tool ever made, and the full extent of its benefits
– and risks – is yet to be discovered. We believe that it is just as important to question how we
should think about this tool, what it means for humanity and our values, and how we ought to use
it. Unprompted is intended to engage with these questions rigorously.

This issue brings work from three sections together. Philosophy examines the
phenomenological implications of AI and how it changes the way we think about ethics. Culture
& Aesthetics investigates how AI may be reshaping cultural value and what this means for public
discourse. Society, Politics & Law explores the role of AI in medical expertise, market pricing, and
policing. What unites these articles is the refusal to treat AI as simply miraculous or malevolent.
Each looks beyond sensationalist headlines and interrogates what really is changing, and what
we ought to do about it.

We sincerely thank our writers, our editors, our peer reviewers, and the rest of the team for their
hard work to make this project happen. Lastly, we thank you, the reader, for engaging with
questions of tomorrow that we need to answer today.


----

We are looking to build a mag-journal at the intersection of Artificial intelligence and philosophy. There are policy journals that discuss AI, and there are philosophy journals that briefly mention AI. We think the area that bridges the two is nascent and we want to promote discourse within it. 

The timeline of the journal will be as follows: we aim to publish by the end of September 2026, with approximately four months allocated for writing and article development. The AI and Phil society is a ratified society next year, so we will have funding for the magazine from KCLSU.


----
The unprompted journal is an initiative spawned out of the Artificial Intelligence and Philosophy society here at King's. 

Since its establishment last year, we have built a dedicated community of over 580 members and hosted events with an average of 100+ attendees. They have featured speakers such as David Leslie (Director of Ethics and Responsible Innovation Research at The Alan Turing Institute), Jack Stilgoe (Professor of Science and Technology Policy at UCL), and Philip Treleaven (former Director of the UK Centre for Financial Computing). In recognition of our contribution to the field, we were recently honoured to be named “Initiative of the Year” at King’s College London.
